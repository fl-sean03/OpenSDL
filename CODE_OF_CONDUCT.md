# Community code of conduct

## Our standard

OpenSDL is committed to a professional, inclusive, and technically rigorous community. Participation
is open regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity or
expression, experience level, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance,
race, caste, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

Expected behavior includes:

- treating people with respect and critiquing ideas or implementations rather than individuals;
- welcoming questions, disagreement, and correction offered in good faith;
- giving and accepting specific, evidence-based feedback;
- respecting privacy, confidentiality, attribution, and safety boundaries;
- disclosing conflicts of interest and correcting mistakes openly; and
- prioritizing human safety and research integrity over speed or status.

Unacceptable behavior includes harassment, discrimination, threats, sexualized attention, deliberate
intimidation, doxxing, sustained disruption, retaliation, or knowingly encouraging unsafe or deceptive
scientific practice.

## Scope

This policy applies in project repositories, discussions, reviews, issue trackers, events, and other
spaces where someone represents OpenSDL. It also applies to private conduct that directly threatens
the safety of project participants or the integrity of the project.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to
[florezsean0822@gmail.com](mailto:florezsean0822@gmail.com) with `OpenSDL conduct` in the subject.
Reports will be acknowledged, handled as confidentially as practical, and shared only with people
needed to investigate or act. Security vulnerabilities belong in the private channel described in
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), not in a conduct report or public issue.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may request a correction or apology, remove content, issue a warning, limit participation,
temporarily suspend access, or permanently ban a participant. Responses will consider impact,
context, recurrence, cooperation, and the safety of affected people. Retaliation against a reporter or
participant in an investigation is itself a violation.

A maintainer named in a report must recuse themselves when another suitable reviewer is available.
When no conflict-free maintainer is available, the project will seek a neutral external reviewer before
taking a non-urgent final action. Immediate protective action may be taken when necessary to prevent
harm, preserve evidence, or secure project infrastructure.
